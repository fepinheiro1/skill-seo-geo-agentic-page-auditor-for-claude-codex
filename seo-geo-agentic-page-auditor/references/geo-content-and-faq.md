## Intent and page roles

Assign one primary intent and one canonical role to each page. Distinguish:

- definition and education;
- problem and diagnosis;
- product or solution;
- platform or integration;
- comparison and decision;
- evidence, case study, or research;
- transaction or account action.

Avoid near-duplicate pages competing for the same query with only swapped nouns. Either add distinct value for the audience/platform or consolidate.

## Groundable information

AI retrieval systems benefit from discrete claims that can be supported and verified. Build sections around:

- a direct statement;
- scope and conditions;
- evidence or source;
- date or version where freshness matters;
- limitations and exceptions;
- a concrete example.

Do not invent statistics, customers, experiments, authors, reviews, or citations. Mark estimates as estimates and explain the method.

## Answer blocks

Place a concise answer immediately after a question-like heading when that serves the reader. Follow it with depth. Strong blocks often use:

1. a one- or two-sentence answer;
2. a short list, table, formula, or sequence;
3. evidence and context;
4. an explicit limitation;
5. links to deeper first-party or authoritative sources.

Do not fragment prose only to imitate snippets. Preserve natural reading and subject expertise.

## Evidence and provenance

- Prefer primary sources for technical, legal, medical, financial, scientific, and statistical claims.
- Link claims near their supporting source.
- Expose author, reviewer, publication date, and modification date when truthful and useful.
- Explain original methods, sample size, period, and definitions.
- Keep citations live and replace stale sources.
- Separate source-backed fact from analysis or inference.

## Entity consistency

Use stable names for the organization, product, person, place, platform, and concept across:

- H1 and body;
- title and description;
- image alt/caption and filenames;
- JSON-LD names, URLs, IDs, and `sameAs`;
- social card copy;
- breadcrumbs and internal anchors.

Add disambiguating context naturally. Do not repeat keywords mechanically.

## FAQ quality

Use FAQs to resolve real follow-up questions, objections, definitions, compatibility, cost model, setup, security, limitations, and next steps.

Each answer should:

- answer in its first sentence;
- be understandable out of context;
- name the relevant entity or subject;
- include conditions or limitations where needed;
- link to a deeper canonical page when the topic deserves one;
- remain visible in the page HTML, including when presented in an accordion.

Avoid duplicate FAQ sets across many pages. Tailor questions to page intent. There is no universal minimum word count. A short answer can be excellent; a long answer can be evasive.

FAQPage JSON-LD must match visible questions and answers. Google generally limits FAQ rich results to authoritative government and health sites, so do not justify FAQ work by promising expanded Google snippets. The content can still improve usability, retrieval, internal linking, and comprehension.

## Content clusters

Connect pages by user journey and meaning, not by dumping unrelated links. A useful cluster commonly links:

- pillar or category;
- problem;
- solution/product;
- platform/integration;
- FAQ or question;
- comparison;
- evidence/case;
- related insight.

Use descriptive anchor text. Ensure every strategic page receives at least one crawlable contextual link from another indexable page.

## Freshness and measurement

Update content when the facts or intent change, not merely to change a timestamp. Measure separately:

- crawl and index coverage;
- search impressions, position, and CTR;
- cited pages and grounding queries in supported AI-search reporting;
- referrals from AI search;
- assisted conversions and downstream outcomes.

Citation count is not the same as rank, authority, or conversion. Document what each metric actually proves.
